## Main result: non-separability

The almost mirror-symmetric ±225 score swings demonstrate that the two good matched solutions occupy very different, strongly correlated CMB degeneracy manifolds. The model-sector switch cannot be meaningfully interpreted as a small additive 'intrinsic RTK likelihood effect' at a single fixed standard-parameter point.

In particular, it would be scientifically wrong to say either:

- 'RTK intrinsically improves Planck by 225' because the RTK anchor favors RTK, or
- 'RTK intrinsically worsens Planck by 223' because the LCDM anchor favors LCDM.

Both statements are anchor-dependent. The correct statement is that changing the RTK/LCDM sector requires a **coherent reorganization of the standard cosmological parameters**, especially to remain on the high-l CMB acoustic/shape degeneracy manifold.

## Relation to the reoptimized current points

After each model is allowed to move to its own matched local center, the huge fixed-anchor Planck discrepancy collapses to only

`Delta(-2 log L Planck) = -0.331122` (RTK minus LCDM),

while the total provisional gap is only `+0.366590`, generated by BOSS and partly compensated by Planck+Pantheon.

Therefore parameter retuning is not a small correction to the model-sector effect; it is an essential part of obtaining a viable RTK cosmology.

## Physical interpretation

The theory-output cross-anchor atlas already showed percent-level CMB spectral differences at fixed shared parameters and strong cancellation after retuning. The exact likelihood calculation now confirms that the high-l Planck likelihood is exceptionally sensitive to the correlated model+parameter combination. This is a stronger statement than a simple selected-multipole residual comparison.

The relevant scientific question for future inference is therefore the geometry of the **joint model-parameter degeneracy manifold**, not an additive decomposition into 'RTK sector contribution' plus 'standard-parameter contribution'.

## Consequence for future work

1. Do not use fixed-anchor score differences as a preference statistic.
2. Any posterior/model-comparison calculation must allow full correlated reoptimization/sampling of the shared parameters within each model.
3. The matched minima and their stationarity remain the correct objects for raw Delta S.
4. To understand what breaks the degeneracy, combine high-l CMB information with the late-time BOSS geometry-growth mode already identified in the BOSS PCA audit.
5. A local Jacobian/Fisher analysis around each model's own minimum can be useful for mapping the tangent degeneracy directions, but it must be model-specific.
